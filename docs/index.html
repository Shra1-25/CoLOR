The Background-Shift Trap

A new class shows up after deployment. The obvious detector : train a classifier to tell training data from deployment data, and call the deployment-looking points novel : can score worse than a coin flip, while a constrained rule on the very same data clears 0.90. Both find the same amount of novel-class signal. The difference is what else they are forced to buy.

4 · the mechanism

Where the weight goes

The punchline. Both rules pick up the same novel-class signal. The discriminator must also separate training from deployment by a full margin, and the cheapest way is to lean hard on the background-shift direction. That extra lean lifts familiar points above novel ones.

Theorem 1's stated conditions

The sufficient conditions of Theorem 2 (TMLR 05/2026), eqs. (8)–(11). They are not necessary : the separation above is real wherever the two big numbers say so, whether or not these boxes are green.

These boxes will not all go green here, and that is expected rather than a failure. Chaining (9) into (10) caps rη ≤ 1/(16√N_T,1) ≤ 1/16, which against (8)'s floor 16/√N_T needs N_T ≥ 66,049 : with N_S ≥ N_T that is a 142 GB Gram matrix, against 6.5 MB here. The theorem and this simulator therefore show the same phenomenon from two different corners: panel 6 draws both.

What is being solved. Source P_S = N(μ, σ²(I − η̂η̂ᵀ)), shifted known class P_T,0 = N(−μ, σ²(I − η̂η̂ᵀ)), novel class P_T,1 = N(−η, σ²(I − μ̂μ̂ᵀ)), with μ ⟂ η and σ = 1/√d. Both detectors are the max-margin solutions of eq. (2): the discriminator demands margin 1 on both sets; CoLOR demands margin 1 on the deployment set and only the correct side (margin 0) on the training set. Because every quantity depends on the data solely through the two informative coordinates and the Gram matrix of the orthogonal noise : exactly σ²·Wishart(d−2, I_N) : the full d-dimensional problem is simulated here in O(N²) without approximation. Validated against the explicit d-dimensional solve and against empirical AUROC on fresh samples.
